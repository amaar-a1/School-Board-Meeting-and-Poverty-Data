      Map of school districts

        Loading district borders…

        Hover over or tap a district to see its figures; click to zoom in. District borders are the Census Bureau's 2024 cartographic boundaries (simplified). Unified and elementary districts are shown; in states that also have separate high school districts, tick “Secondary districts” to draw those on top. Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands and American Samoa have one territory-wide district each, so their outline is the whole territory. Vermont's poverty figures are for supervisory unions, so each Vermont town district shows its union's figures (CivicSearch has no Vermont boards; meetings counted from other sites cover only a few unions, and others may post meetings elsewhere); districts merged in 2024 show the merged district's figures on their old borders. High school pieces of districts show their parent district. Districts the Census poverty file leaves out (Defense Department base schools, districts with no schools of their own, a few tiny districts) show their county's all-ages poverty rate, noted in their tooltip. Districts with no meetings found count as 0 (palest blue). Pale green areas are outside any school district, mostly national parks and other federal land. When a state is picked or filters are on, other districts are faded.

        Canada (pick “Canada” above): school board borders come from each province (British Columbia, Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Ontario and Quebec publish them). Nova Scotia, New Brunswick and the Northwest Territories boards are drawn from the counties or regions they serve. Boards that serve a whole province or territory (French-language boards, Prince Edward Island, Newfoundland and Labrador, the Yukon and Nunavut) are drawn as the whole province or territory. Canada's boards overlap: public and Catholic boards, and English and French ones, serve the same places. The map shows the public (or English) board for each place by default (in the Yukon, the Yukon First Nation School Board; in Nunavut, its French board, the only board there); tick “Overlapping Canadian boards” to draw the Catholic and French-language boards on top. Boards with no meetings found are drawn too, in the palest blue. Land outside every public board (most of Ontario's Far North: unorganized land and First Nations reserves, whose schools are run by First Nations or small school authorities that publish no borders) is drawn as “Rest of Ontario (no school board)”, and likewise “Rest of Alberta” (mostly national parks and First Nations reserves). Their rate comes from the census areas inside them (Statistics Canada has no low-income figures for reserves, so it mostly reflects unorganized areas), or the province-wide rate where there are too few children; meetings are 0. These are on the map only, not in the provinces' tables, charts or totals. Canada's colors use the share of children 0–17 in low income (2021 Census, LIM-AT), which isn't the same measure as the US child poverty rate.
