# School Board Meetings & Poverty Data Prototype App

A website that shows the project's school board meeting and poverty data with summary stats, an interactive chart, and a searchable, sortable table you can download.

- **Data:** [`docs/data/national.csv`](docs/data/national.csv), every school district in the 50 states and DC, with meeting counts refreshed daily from CivicSearch. [`docs/data/territories.csv`](docs/data/territories.csv) covers the U.S. territories.
- **Sources:** [sources document](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1U5bDB31MpETIwG8Q8xZ7MZdH9oNooY_W6TnpSkNdBFk/edit?usp=drivesdk)

## How it works

The site is a static page in `docs/` that reads its data from CSV files in `docs/data/`. The workflow `.github/workflows/pages.yml` downloads CivicSearch's district list, rebuilds `docs/data/national.csv` with `data-prep/build_national.py`, commits it if it changed, and deploys the site to GitHub Pages. It runs on every push to `main`, once a day, and on demand (**Actions → Update data and deploy website → Run workflow**).

| File | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `docs/config.js` | Which CSV files to show, links |
| `docs/sources.js` | Sources listed on the page |
| `docs/state-rules.js` | State rules on recording or streaming meetings |
| `docs/data/national.csv` | US data shown on the site (meeting counts refreshed daily) |
| `docs/data/territories.csv` | U.S. territories data |
| `docs/data/data.csv` | The project spreadsheet's Texas data as of October 29, 2026 (kept for reference; the site no longer uses it) |
| `docs/app.js`, `docs/style.css`, `docs/index.html` | The site itself |

### Details

- **National data:** `national.csv` has every school district in the 50 states and DC from the Census SAIPE 2024 file (13,131; Puerto Rico, the file's only territory, is in the territories view instead), with NCES 2023 enrollment and locale and the meeting count from CivicSearch's district list (the rest show 0). The workflow reruns `data-prep/build_national.py` daily with a fresh CivicSearch list and the Census and NCES files saved in `data-prep/inputs/` (the NCES file is trimmed to the fields the script uses; `nces_fallback.json` fills enrollment or locale missing from the 2023 directory with the latest 2018–2022 value). About 130 districts still have no enrollment, mostly Vermont supervisory unions (NCES counts students in their member districts), districts on military bases, and districts served by a neighbor; the By district size tab leaves them out and says how many. Five districts have no children ages 5–17 in the Census file (tiny unorganized areas and island districts in Maine, Ohio and Oregon); they show their county's all-ages poverty rate from SAIPE 2024 (`data-prep/inputs/est24all.txt`), and the `Notes` column says so. If the download fails or looks wrong, the site keeps the last good data. `data-prep/civicsearch_matches.csv` shows which district each CivicSearch entry was matched to.
- **County and notes:** `County` comes from NCES (or, for districts NCES doesn't list, from the district's name or the districts it replaced), so districts with the same name in a state can be told apart. `Notes` explains special cases: districts with no school-age children, districts merged in 2024 (enrollment and locale are their predecessors' 2023 figures), districts served by a neighbor (Fairfax City, Emporia City and James City County in Virginia; Lawrence-Nelson and Sandy Creek in Nebraska), New York City (enrollment is the total of NCES's 32 geographic districts), and districts whose enrollment is far above their school-age children because of online or statewide programs (e.g. Hallsville ISD, Texas).
- **Still blank, and why:** every district has a county. 72 have no enrollment and 17 no locale, each with a note: Defense Department base school systems (NCES doesn't count their students; DoDEA does), districts with no schools of their own (their students attend other districts), Maine's unorganized territories (served by a statewide program NCES reports as a whole), pieces of Massachusetts towns, and McDermitt, Oregon (its students attend school in Nevada). Cherokee Central Schools (NC) and Highland Park and Muskegon Heights (MI) take their enrollment from the Bureau of Indian Education and charter academy systems NCES lists separately.
- **CivicSearch entries not matched:** 7 of CivicSearch's 1,213 entries aren't geographic school districts (county offices of education in California and Arizona, the Harris County Department of Education, a Colorado administrative unit, a charter school and a vocational district), so they have no Census poverty row and aren't counted.
- **Texas:** the Texas view is the national rows for Texas, like every other state. On October 29, 2026 these matched the project spreadsheet's numbers for all 1,017 districts (the names use the Census's full form, e.g. "Austin Independent School District"). The spreadsheet is no longer downloaded; its last copy is `docs/data/data.csv`.
- **United States / state menu:** a button and a menu at the top switch every view between the national data and any single state or DC. On the By district size tab, each state shows its rules from `docs/state-rules.js`, and states with an enrollment cutoff (Texas, Oregon) get a count of the districts it covers. The last menu entry, U.S. territories, shows Puerto Rico, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands, the U.S. Virgin Islands and American Samoa together from `docs/data/territories.csv`. That file lists each territory's school system, its board and whether the board posts meetings online, with NCES schools and enrollment (2023; Guam 2021 and American Samoa 2020, their latest NCES enrollment), and children ages 5–17 and child poverty: Census SAIPE 2024 for Puerto Rico, and the 2020 Census Demographic and Housing Characteristics files (poverty in 2019 by age, children in households) for the others, with the Virgin Islands split by island to match its two districts. `data-prep/territory_poverty.md` explains how those were extracted. CivicSearch doesn't cover the territories, so their `Available Board Meetings` are the board meetings recorded on the boards' YouTube channels (counted October 30, 2026; Facebook-only recordings couldn't be checked), and `Locale` is the NCES 2023 locale. With those, the territories view has the same sections as a state. Its sources show only on its own Sources tab, and the state rules sources show only for the states.
- **Urban sections:** the **Urban & suburban districts only** section uses the `Locale` column (or `Urban`, `Urban District`, `Urbanicity`, `Urban/Rural`). A district is included if that cell says `Urban` or `Suburban` (also `Yes`, `True`, `1`, `X`, or an NCES "City"/"Suburb" locale). `Town`, `Rural` or a blank cell are left out. An **Urban districts only** section does the same for `Urban` (NCES "City") districts alone, without suburbs. NCES locale codes 11–13 are City (Urban), 21–23 Suburb (Suburban), 31–33 Town, 41–43 Rural.
- **Filters** (Overview and Map tabs): locale, with/without available meetings, a child poverty range and the district size tiers. The data table, its CSV download and the map follow them (the map fades districts that don't match); the summary cards and charts use all districts.
- **Ask about the data** (`docs/assistant.js`): a question panel opened from the button at the bottom right. It is not an AI model: it recognizes common kinds of questions and answers them in the browser from the site's data — a district's figures (“What is the poverty rate in Austin ISD?”), highest/lowest lists (“most meetings in Ohio”, “top 3 largest districts in California”), counts (“How many rural districts in Iowa have meetings?”, “districts with over 30% poverty”), averages for a state or the territories, side-by-side state comparisons, state rules, and where the data comes from. Unrecognized questions get example questions instead.
- **Map tab** (`docs/map.js`): every district's border, colored by child poverty rate or available board meetings, with a tooltip of its figures. It zooms to the state picked in the menu and fades the rest. Borders are the Census Bureau's 2024 cartographic boundary files (unified, elementary and secondary districts, plus territory outlines), simplified with mapshaper into TopoJSON in `docs/data/map/` (about 3.7 MB). Each border's `GEOID` is the district's `District ID`. `docs/data/map/aliases.json` (built by `data-prep/build_map_aliases.py`) lets borders with no row of their own show other districts' figures, on the map only: Vermont town districts show their supervisory union's figures (Census reports Vermont poverty by union), and the old pieces of districts merged in 2024 (Connecticut Region 20, Henry Hudson Regional in New Jersey, St. Anne Unit 24 in Illinois, Barnwell County in South Carolina) show the merged district; South Central Nebraska Unified 5 shows Lawrence-Nelson and Sandy Creek combined. Grade-range pieces of districts (e.g. “Hamilton Unified School District (9-12)”) show their parent district, matched by name. Eight districts the Census poverty file leaves out (Defense Department base schools, districts with no schools of their own, Tanana City AK and Spring Creek ISD TX) show their county's all-ages poverty rate, with a note. Every district in the data appears on the map and no border is gray; on the meetings map, a district with no meetings found counts as 0.
- **By district size** groups districts by NCES student enrollment (2023): Low 0–499, Medium 500–9,999, Big 10,000–49,999, Large 50,000–99,999, Huge 100,000+. For each tier it compares child poverty for districts with vs. without available meetings, the share of districts with meetings, average meetings, meetings per 10,000 students and meetings per poverty point.
- Earlier spreadsheet helpers are kept in `data-prep/`: `district_locales.csv` (each spreadsheet district's NCES match, locale and enrollment) and the `*_column_for_sheet.txt` files.
- In the repo, go to **Settings → Pages** and set **Source** to **GitHub Actions** (if the first deploy didn't enable it automatically).

### Run locally

```sh
cd docs && python3 -m http.server 8000
# open http://localhost:8000
```
